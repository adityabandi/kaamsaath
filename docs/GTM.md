# Jothe: low-cost marketplace launch plan

## Summary

Do not launch a city. Win one dense service cell: a 3-5 km cluster where workers can travel cheaply, hirers share local trust networks, and support can reach a failed job quickly. Build liquidity around urgent, legible repair jobs first. Use trusted offline nodes and WhatsApp forwarding for worker supply; use residents, repeat hirers, apartment/shop clusters, and completed-job proof for demand. Spend only behind a channel after it produces completed, paid, undisputed jobs.

This is a proposed operating model. No outreach, ad spend, partnership, ambassador appointment, or public launch is approved.

## Recommended wedge

### Pilot cell
Start with **East Bengaluru: Indiranagar, Domlur, Jeevan Bhima Nagar, and the adjoining HAL/Kodihalli edge**, subject to field validation.

Why this is the leading hypothesis:
- contiguous, dense, WhatsApp-heavy household and small-business demand
- mixed housing, apartments, shops, clinics, offices, and restaurants
- short worker travel distances and many repeatable local tasks
- multilingual labor market where a voice/local-language product has a reason to exist
- enough willingness to pay for reliability without requiring a premium managed-service model

The locality choice is a hypothesis, not a claim based on platform data. Validate it against 25 worker and 25 hirer interviews, local wage levels, worker residence/travel patterns, job frequency, and support access before activation.

### First categories
1. Electrical repair
2. Plumbing
3. Appliance diagnosis/minor repair
4. Painting touch-ups and one-day jobs

These have visible problems, describable scopes, meaningful urgency, and relatively fast completion loops. Start with electrical and plumbing; add the next category only after the first two have reliable response and completion.

Avoid at pilot: childcare, elder care, domestic work inside homes without stronger safeguards, driving, security, high-risk construction, gas, structural work, and jobs needing licensed sign-off.

## Liquidity sequence

### Cell activation target
Do not count registrations as supply. A worker is active only after onboarding, availability confirmation, and one successful test response.

For one cell and two categories, aim for:
- 15-20 active workers per category
- at least 8 available in each common time window
- no single worker receiving more than 20% of offers
- 40-60 seeded hirers likely to produce the first 25 real jobs
- a human operator available during all promised service hours

### Supply first, but only just enough
Days 1-10:
- build a private roster through trusted nodes
- map skill, locality, language, travel radius, tools, hours, wage preference, and references
- run comprehension tests using sample Fair Job Cards
- pay nothing for sign-up; if incentives are tested, make them earned by a completed, undisputed first job
- pause onboarding once the availability target is reached, then activate demand

Demand begins before supply is "complete." The first real hirer cohort should be recruited when at least 8 suitable workers per category/time band reliably answer test offers.

## Worker acquisition

### Channel priority
1. **Worker-to-worker invitations after a good job.** The worker shares a language-specific WhatsApp card or voice note. Attribution uses a code, not contact-list access.
2. **Trusted trade nodes:** electrical/plumbing supply shops, hardware stores, repair-parts shops, tool rental counters, tea stalls near labor nodes, and respected senior workers.
3. **Worker institutions:** skilling centers, ITIs, worker collectives, NGOs, unions, and community organizations. Partnership language must promise no commission and explain data/safety limits plainly.
4. **Field ambassador:** one credible local person per micro-area, paid for verified onboarding quality and 30-day active supply, never raw registrations.
5. **Small WhatsApp groups:** admins forward an approved job/service card; Jothe never scrapes groups or imports members.

### Worker pitch
Lead with: full agreed wage, jobs near chosen areas, terms before travel, hirers rated too, no app, and ability to stop alerts. Do not lead with "AI," high income promises, or a referral reward.

### Offline kit
- A6 bilingual card with QR + missed-call/WhatsApp number
- 30-second local-language audio explainer
- sample Fair Job Card
- "workers pay ₹0" card
- fraud warning: Jothe never asks for OTP, UPI PIN, or payment to unlock work

## Demand acquisition

### Founding hirers
Recruit a narrow cohort with repeat/high-signal demand:
- apartment committee members and building managers
- independent landlords/property managers
- neighborhood shop/clinic/restaurant operators
- home-office operators and resident connectors
- local repair-material shops willing to mention the service to buyers who need labor

Do not sign a broad RWA partnership first. Start with one building champion and a private pilot code. A formal logo partnership adds negotiation and expectation before liquidity exists.

### Demand loops
- A completed job produces a private WhatsApp receipt/job card and a one-tap "hire again" route.
- Hirers can save a worker, but the worker chooses whether repeat requests bypass open matching.
- The payment confirmation asks for a referral only after a completed, undisputed job.
- A building can get a trusted local pool after three completed jobs, giving it a reason to consolidate usage.
- Job cards can carry a neutral "Found through Jothe" share card, without exposing wage, address, or either party.
- Reactivate only when there is a useful trigger: maintenance season, saved worker availability, or an unfinished repeat task, within consent and template rules.

## Referral economics

### Recommended first test
Do not pay cash for invites. Test a **completion-gated fee credit** on the hirer side and a **worker utility benefit**:
- hirer referrer: one platform-fee credit after the referred hirer's first paid, undisputed job
- new hirer: first platform fee waived or reduced
- worker referrer: priority verification appointment, work-card upgrade, or a small fixed reward only after the referred worker completes two undisputed jobs

Never make worker ranking depend on referrals.

### Cash test only after baseline retention
If cash is needed, start with a proposed cap of ₹150-₹250 per activated worker or completed first hirer job, and model it against 90-day contribution. This is an experiment range, not approved spend or a current market price.

### Fraud controls
- reward only after completion + bilateral amount confirmation + cooling period
- unique referrer/referred phone, device, UPI beneficiary, and job parties
- household/address and repeated-pair velocity checks
- no self-referral, circular chains, or multiple rewards for the same party
- cap rewards per person/month
- withhold disputed, cancelled, refunded, suspiciously short, or identical-pattern jobs
- manual review before payout in the pilot
- never punish ordinary shared devices or family numbers automatically; flag for review

## Trust and safety as acquisition

Market verifiable product behavior, not "verified professionals" as a blanket claim:
- wage and scope agreed before travel
- exact address hidden until both agree
- mutual payment/safety/respect ratings
- scope changes require a revised wage
- direct record of amount due
- stop alerts at any time
- clear human support hours

Publish no safety-rate claim until there is enough audited data. Never suggest that phone/reference checks guarantee safety.

## Launch operations

### Concierge team
For the first 100 jobs, a human reviews every job before matching, every first-time participant match, material scope changes, cancellations, payment failures, and low-confidence agent response. One operator can own a single cell only if response and case load stay inside the service promise.

### Daily control room
Morning: worker availability sweep and open-job risk.  
During service hours: new job SLA, match batch timing, late/no-show checks, safety queue.  
Evening: payment reconciliation, disputes, worker exposure audit, tomorrow supply gaps.  
Weekly: worker council call, hirer interviews, wage guidance review, channel cohort review, and abuse patterns.

### Service promise for pilot
Set hours rather than pretend 24/7. Proposed test: 8 AM-8 PM local support; urgent same-day matching only while staff is active. This needs user approval before publishing or staffing.

## Retention

### Workers
- preference-aware offers and low notification load
- weekly availability control
- transparent reasons for each offer and non-selection
- portable work card and repeat-hirer relationships
- no ranking penalty for declining
- feedback on profile gaps and local demand
- opt-in weekly summary of earnings and missed-fit categories

### Hirers
- hire-again in one message
- saved scope from prior job, never a prefilled stale address without confirmation
- trusted local pool after successful jobs
- proactive maintenance reminders only with separate consent
- fast recovery after no-show or dispute

## Experiments

Run sequentially inside one cell; randomize only when operations can support both treatments.

| Hypothesis | Test | Primary measure | Stop/keep rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clear wage raises worker response | wage-first vs ordinary job card | qualified interest rate | keep only if completion and disputes do not worsen |
| Three-worker batches are enough | 3 vs 5 eligible workers | time to first qualified interest | choose smaller batch if SLA holds |
| Trade shops produce quality supply | shop cards vs worker referral | completed first jobs per onboarded worker | stop any channel with weak activation after 30 qualified leads |
| Human voice onboarding helps | voice callback vs text flow | completed onboarding and first response | keep if added cost fits activation value |
| No-cash referral works | utility benefit vs no reward | activated referrals | cash only if organic is inadequate |
| Mutual-rating copy builds demand trust | safety/payment message vs convenience message | first completed job | reject copy that harms worker respect or invites overclaiming |

Do not optimize only job fill. Every experiment reports worker effective earnings, travel, payment, safety, cancellation, exposure, and support burden.

## Instrumentation

### Liquidity by cell/category/time band
- active eligible workers
- jobs opened, qualified, offered, interested, agreed, completed
- p50/p90 time to first qualified interest and agreement
- fill and completion rate
- batch expansion rate and offers per completed job
- worker offer exposure distribution

### Worker health
- effective earnings after travel/time
- agreed wage received ratio
- on-time payment
- decline rate without penalty
- 7/30/90-day active retention
- repeat hirer share
- disputes/safety reports and response time

### Acquisition economics
- qualified lead, activated participant, first completed job by source
- field hours and materials by source
- referral liability and fraud loss
- CAC per first completed job, not signup
- 30/90-day completed jobs and contribution by cohort

### Marketplace contribution
`hirer fee revenue - WhatsApp message cost - payment/support/fraud variable cost - local acquisition incentive`

Worker wages are never marketplace revenue. Field-team fixed costs are shown separately and included in cell payback.

## Proposed budget model, uncommitted

A frugal 6-week cell test can be modeled before any spend:

| Line | Lean range (INR) | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Field operator/ambassador time | ₹30k-₹60k | depends on staffing model and city rates |
| Print/QR/translation materials | ₹5k-₹12k | small runs, 2-3 languages |
| Worker research honoraria | ₹15k-₹30k | pay for co-design time, not signups |
| Referral/activation experiment reserve | ₹10k-₹25k | completion-gated only |
| WhatsApp/BSP/message tests | ₹5k-₹15k | re-price from Meta/BSP before approval |
| Local travel/support | ₹8k-₹18k | operator field movement |
| Contingency | ₹7k-₹15k | no automatic use |
| **Modeled total** | **₹80k-₹175k** | planning range, not authorization |

Start closer to the bottom using founder/operator time and no paid ads. Release each next tranche only at a stage gate. Do not buy broad Meta/Google ads in the first cell; they can create demand outside serviceable supply and hide weak local trust.

## Stage gates

### Gate 0: evidence
25 workers + 25 hirers interviewed; wage/scope vocabulary understood; two categories and one cell selected; credible support/safety partners mapped.

### Gate 1: supply response
At least 15 active workers/category, 70%+ test-offer response within the promised window, and no serious comprehension failure.

### Gate 2: first 25 jobs
60%+ qualified jobs fill, 80%+ agreed jobs complete, 90%+ wage received as agreed, all safety cases acknowledged within promise, and no evidence one worker cohort is starved of exposure. Thresholds are proposed and should be adjusted from observed baselines, not marketed.

### Gate 3: repeat liquidity
100 completed jobs, 30%+ hirer repeat within an appropriate need window, 40%+ worker 30-day active retention, support load falling, and a plausible positive variable contribution path.

### Gate 4: adjacent cell
Replicate with the same two categories in one adjoining locality. Expansion succeeds only if the second cell reaches the first cell's liquidity/safety range without borrowing most of its supply.

### Gate 5: category or city expansion
Add one variable at a time: either category or geography. A city launch waits for a repeatable node-acquisition playbook, trained support, multilingual templates, and audited safety operations.

## 30-day operator plan

**Week 1:** field interviews, node map, worker economics, service-cell boundary, category choice.  
**Week 2:** recruit and test 20-30 workers; rehearse job cards, voice, opt-out, and safety flow.  
**Week 3:** invite a private founding-hirer cohort; run first jobs with full human review.  
**Week 4:** inspect every failure, start one acquisition experiment, stop weak sources, and decide Gate 2 trajectory.

## Locked v1 override: Bengaluru home cooks

The user selected part-time recurring home cooks as v1. Earlier electrical/plumbing recommendations are historical hypotheses and do not control the v1 plan.

For v1, liquidity is measured by cell x breakfast/lunch/dinner window x cuisine/task cluster x travel band. Start with paid trials and recurring monthly arrangements. Supply acquisition prioritizes cook networks, worker/community organizations, apartment staff networks and referrals after good paid trials. Demand comes from dense apartment clusters and resident connectors. Do not promise replacement inventory, recruit cleaners under the cook category, or let building gatekeepers set wages.
